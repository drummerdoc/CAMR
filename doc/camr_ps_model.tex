\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This note records the two-phase model \emph{as the code actually solves it},
rather than in its textbook form.  Each equation, closure and source term is
anchored to \code{file:line}.  Runtime options are given with their defaults,
because several of the model's decisions are dials rather than constants.  Two
places where the implementation is known to depart from the model it intends
are flagged explicitly in \S\ref{sec:caveats}.
\end{abstract}

% =====================================================================
\section{State vector}
% =====================================================================

The conserved state carries the two-phase slots
\src{Utils/IndexDefines.H:145--149} alongside CAMR's ordinary single-fluid
slots \src{:17--18,~74--76}:

\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{U} =
  \bigl(\
    \underbrace{\rho,\ \rho\uvec,\ \rho E,\ \rho e,\ T}_{\text{stock CAMR}},\
    \underbrace{\alpha_1,\ \alpha_1\rho_1,\ \alpha_2\rho_2,\
                \alpha_1\rho_1E_1,\ \alpha_2\rho_2E_2}_{\text{PS extension}}\
  \bigr)^{\!\top}.
\end{equation}

Write $m_k=\alpha_k\rho_k$ for the partial masses and
$\mathcal{E}_k=\alpha_k\rho_kE_k$ for the phase total energies, with
$E_k=e_k+\tfrac12|\uvec|^2$.  The volume fractions close as
$\alpha_1+\alpha_2=1$, so only $\alpha_1$ is stored.

\paragraph{The state is deliberately redundant.}
Mixture mass and energy are stored \emph{both} as $\rho,\ \rho E$ \emph{and}
as the phase sums.  The two identities
\begin{equation}
  \rho \;=\; m_1+m_2,
  \qquad
  \rho E \;=\; \mathcal{E}_1+\mathcal{E}_2
  \label{eq:identities}
\end{equation}
are therefore invariants to be \emph{maintained}, not consequences of the
discretisation.  They are re-established every step by
\code{ps\_resync\_mixture\_mass} and \code{ps\_resync\_phase\_energy}
\src{CAMR\_advance.cpp:410--413} and monitored as checks V4 and V5 of
\code{ps\_validate\_state}.  Historically the mass identity drifted at
$\sim\!0.4\,\%$ per production run before anyone was watching it.

\section{Governing equations}
% =====================================================================

With a single mixture velocity $\uvec$ (velocity equilibrium) and
instantaneous mechanical relaxation, the system solved is

\begin{align}
  \dd{\alpha_1}{t} + \uvec\!\cdot\!\nabla\alpha_1
    &= \mathcal{S}_{\alpha}, \label{eq:alpha}\\[2pt]
  \dd{m_1}{t} + \nabla\!\cdot\!\bigl(m_1\uvec\bigr)
    &= \Gamma, \label{eq:m1}\\[2pt]
  \dd{m_2}{t} + \nabla\!\cdot\!\bigl(m_2\uvec\bigr)
    &= -\,\Gamma, \label{eq:m2}\\[2pt]
  \dd{(\rho\uvec)}{t}
    + \nabla\!\cdot\!\bigl(\rho\,\uvec\!\otimes\!\uvec + P\,\Ivec\bigr)
    &= \mathbf{0}, \label{eq:mom}\\[2pt]
  \dd{\mathcal{E}_1}{t}
    + \nabla\!\cdot\!\bigl(\mathcal{E}_1\uvec + \alpha_1P_1\uvec\bigr)
    &= -\,P_{\!I}\,\dd{\alpha_1}{t}
       \;+\;\mathcal{Q}\;+\;\Gamma H_{\!I}, \label{eq:E1}\\[2pt]
  \dd{\mathcal{E}_2}{t}
    + \nabla\!\cdot\!\bigl(\mathcal{E}_2\uvec + \alpha_2P_2\uvec\bigr)
    &= +\,P_{\!I}\,\dd{\alpha_1}{t}
       \;-\;\mathcal{Q}\;-\;\Gamma H_{\!I}, \label{eq:E2}
\end{align}
%
together with the mixture total energy in strict conservation form,
\begin{equation}
  \dd{(\rho E)}{t}
    + \nabla\!\cdot\!\bigl[(\rho E + P)\,\uvec\bigr] \;=\; 0,
  \label{eq:etot}
\end{equation}
which is the quantity that is fluxed and refluxed, with
\eqref{eq:identities} tying it to \eqref{eq:E1}--\eqref{eq:E2}.  The mixture
pressure is the volume-fraction average
\begin{equation}
  P \;=\; \alpha_1P_1 + \alpha_2P_2,
  \qquad\qquad \src{PS\_hllc.H:289}
  \label{eq:pmix}
\end{equation}
and the interfacial pressure is the arithmetic mean
\begin{equation}
  P_{\!I} \;=\; \tfrac12\,(P_1+P_2).
  \qquad\qquad \src{hem\_pelanti\_shyue.H:2157}
  \label{eq:pint}
\end{equation}

Equations \eqref{eq:alpha}, \eqref{eq:E1} and \eqref{eq:E2} are
\emph{not} in divergence form: $\alpha_1$ is advected rather than fluxed, and
the phase energies carry the non-conservative product
$P_{\!I}\,\partial_t\alpha_1$.  That is the entire reason the code uses
wave propagation rather than flux differencing for those three slots
(\S\ref{sec:disc}).

\section{Equation of state and the branch-locked contract}
% =====================================================================

The reference backend is Peng--Robinson for pure CO$_2$
($T_c=304.13$~K, $P_c=7.3773\times10^6$~Pa, $\omega=0.22394$).  Queries are
\emph{branch-locked}: the caller nominates the phase, and the EOS returns that
branch, continued metastably past its saturation limit if necessary.  It never
auto-detects, because a nominal vapour sitting at compressed-liquid density
would otherwise return the liquid root and drive the relaxation toward an
unphysical equilibrium.

The corresponding anti-requirement is load-bearing:
\begin{quote}
  \emph{the hydraulic path must not ask the EOS about a mixture state.}
\end{quote}
For a two-phase cell the pair $(\rho,e)$ combines the light phase's volume
with the heavy phase's mass and need not be a single-fluid state at all, so the
inversion may have no root.  Temperature inversion brackets
$T\in[1,5000]$~K and \emph{refuses} outside it rather than returning the
nearest reachable state.

\section{Source terms}
\label{sec:sources}
% =====================================================================

The right-hand sides are applied in a split sequence, per Strang substep
\src{CAMR\_advance.cpp:440--452}:
\begin{equation}
  \text{hydro}\ \longrightarrow\
  \underbrace{\text{mechanical}}_{\text{instantaneous}}\ \longrightarrow\
  \underbrace{\text{thermal}}_{\text{rate }1/\theta}\ \longrightarrow\
  \underbrace{\text{flash}}_{\text{nucleation}}\ \longrightarrow\
  \underbrace{\text{mass transfer}}_{\text{rate }1/\tau_{\!m}}
  \label{eq:chain}
\end{equation}
with a mechanical re-projection after each of the last two.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Mechanical relaxation: $\mathcal{S}_\alpha$}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

Pressure equilibrium is \textbf{instantaneous by model closure}: $\alpha_1$ is
the free variable and is driven until $P_1=P_2$.  It is a constraint, not a
rate.  The accompanying interfacial work is applied to the phase energies
\src{hem\_pelanti\_shyue.H:2223--2225}:
\begin{equation}
  e_1 \;\mapsto\; e_1 - \frac{P_{\!I}\,\Delta\alpha_1}{\alpha_1\rho_1},
  \qquad
  e_2 \;\mapsto\; e_2 + \frac{P_{\!I}\,\Delta\alpha_1}{\alpha_2\rho_2},
  \label{eq:mechwork}
\end{equation}
equal and opposite in $\mathcal{E}_k$, so mixture energy is preserved
exactly.  This is the discrete realisation of $\mp P_{\!I}\partial_t\alpha_1$
in \eqref{eq:E1}--\eqref{eq:E2}.  The Newton step on $\alpha_1$ is capped at a
fraction of $\min(\alpha_1,1-\alpha_1)$ and restricted to the interval where
both $\rho_k$ remain inside the EOS domain.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Thermal relaxation: $\mathcal{Q}$}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

Finite-rate, isochoric, at the $\alpha_1$ the mechanical pass chose.  It
redistributes the energy split only, driving $T_1\!\to\!T_2$ at rate
$1/\theta$ \src{hem\_pelanti\_shyue.H, ps\_iso\_thermal\_relax\_cell\_finite}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{E}_k^{\,n+1}
    = \mathcal{E}_k^{\,*}
    + \bigl(\mathcal{E}_k^{\,\mathrm{eq}}-\mathcal{E}_k^{\,*}\bigr)
      \Bigl(1-e^{-\Delta t/\theta}\Bigr),
  \label{eq:thermal}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{eq}}$ is the isochoric $T_1\!=\!T_2$ state.  Note
the form: this is an \emph{exponential relaxation onto a precomputed
equilibrium}, with $\theta$ supplied by the user
(\code{CAMR.ps\_theta\_tau}).

The leg is gated by a two-phase coexistence test --- both phase temperatures
strictly inside $(T_{\mathrm{triple}},T_{\mathrm{crit}}) = (216.592,\,304.13)$~K
--- shared with mass transfer and the flash.  \code{CAMR.ps\_coexist\_action}
(default $0$) selects what happens when a phase leaves that band: $0$ silent
no-op, $1$ abort, $2$ run the thermal leg for low-side exits, $3$ run it for
any exit.

Optionally $\theta$ may be taken per cell from the homogeneous-relaxation
correlation of Downar-Zapolski \emph{et al.} (1996)
(\code{CAMR.ps\_theta\_model}$=1$, default off):
\begin{equation}
  \theta = \theta_0\,\alpha_v^{-0.54}\,\psi^{-1.76},
  \qquad
  \psi = \frac{\bigl|P_{\mathrm{sat}}(T_1)-P\bigr|}{P_c-P_{\mathrm{sat}}(T_1)},
  \qquad \theta_0 = 3.84\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{s}.
  \label{eq:hrm}
\end{equation}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Mass transfer: $\Gamma$ and $H_{\!I}$}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

Driven by the Gibbs free-energy difference $g_1-g_2$ with $g_k=h_k-T_ks_k$,
and integrated as an exact relaxation onto the instantaneous mass-transfer
equilibrium:
\begin{equation}
  \delta m \;=\; \Bigl(1-e^{-\Delta t/\tau_{\!m}}\Bigr)\,\delta m_{\mathrm{eq}},
  \qquad
  \delta m_{\mathrm{eq}}:\ g_1 = g_2 ,
  \label{eq:mt}
\end{equation}
so $\Gamma = -\delta m/\Delta t$ in \eqref{eq:m1}.  The target
$\delta m_{\mathrm{eq}}$ is found by a Newton iteration on $\delta m$.  The
form \eqref{eq:mt} is unconditionally stable and cannot overshoot
$\delta m_{\mathrm{eq}}$ --- \emph{provided} the target is the equilibrium of
the path actually integrated; see \S\ref{sec:caveats}.

Transferred mass carries the interface total enthalpy
\begin{equation}
  H_{\!I} = h_{\!I} + \tfrac12|\uvec|^2,
  \qquad
  h_{\!I} =
  \begin{cases}
    (1-w)\,h_1 + w\,h_2, & w \ge 0 \quad(w=\tfrac12 \text{ default: mean}),\\[2pt]
    h_1 \ \text{if}\ \delta m>0,\ \ h_2 \ \text{otherwise}, & w<0 \quad(\text{upwind}),
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:carrier}
\end{equation}
selected by \code{CAMR.ps\_mt\_h\_weight}.

\paragraph{Volume co-moves with the mass (E.1).}
Under the presence model the transfer moves volume at the \emph{donor's own}
density:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\alpha_1 \;=\; -\,\frac{\delta m}{\rho_1}.
  \label{eq:E1rule}
\end{equation}
Two consequences make this the derived choice rather than a convenience.  The
donor's density is then \emph{exactly} invariant,
$\rho_1' = (m_1-\delta m)/(\alpha_1-\delta m/\rho_1) = \rho_1$, so it can never
leave the EOS domain from below; and the receiver's density becomes a convex
combination (a mediant) of $\rho_2$ and $\rho_1$, so it can never leave from
above.  The proof is rate-independent: even $\delta m = m_1$ in one step lands
$\alpha_1$ exactly on zero.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Flash: nucleation only}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

The flash is \textbf{not} a conversion operator.  It seeds a minority phase up
to $\alpha_{\mathrm{birth}} = 2\times10^{-2}$ and declines once that level is
reached \src{hem\_pelanti\_shyue.H:3778}:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_2 \;\mapsto\; \alpha_2 + \beta\,\bigl(\alpha_{\mathrm{birth}}-\alpha_2\bigr),
  \qquad
  \text{return if } \alpha_2^{\,\mathrm{new}} \le \alpha_2 ,
\end{equation}
with $\beta$ a blend of a metastability window, a dominance ramp and
$\Delta t/\tau_f$.  Newborn mass is injected at the \emph{saturation} density
$\rho_{\mathrm{sat}}$, which is \eqref{eq:E1rule} run backwards, so birth and
death are the same operator with opposite sign.  Past
$\alpha_{\mathrm{birth}}$, conversion is mass transfer's job alone.

% =====================================================================
\section{Presence: when a phase has a state at all}
% =====================================================================

The intensive quantities are quotients with \emph{different} denominators,
\begin{equation}
  \rho_k = \frac{m_k}{\alpha_k},
  \qquad
  e_k = \frac{\mathcal{E}_k}{m_k} - \tfrac12|\uvec|^2,
\end{equation}
so a test on one does not license the other.  Because the hydro advances $m_k$
with an absolute error set by the \emph{mixture} scale, $\rho_k$ inherits a
relative error $\sim\eta/\alpha_k$.  A phase is therefore assigned one of three
regimes by $\alpha_k$ alone --- no auxiliary flag array, so the regime cannot
fall out of step with the state:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
regime & $\alpha_k$ & meaning \\
\midrule
\textsc{absent} & exactly $0$
  & no intensive quantity is ever formed; the cell is single-phase Euler \\
\textsc{corridor} & $(\alpha_{\mathrm{van}},\alpha_{\mathrm{cond}})$
  & transport-only: advects, but no EOS query, no relaxation, no transfer \\
\textsc{independent} & $[\alpha_{\mathrm{cond}},1-\alpha_{\mathrm{cond}}]$
  & a full six-equation phase; every operator acts \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
with
$\alpha_{\mathrm{cond}}=10^{-2}$ (a \emph{conditioning} bound, derived as
$\eta_{\max}/\varepsilon$ from a measured per-step $\eta_{\max}=5.2\times10^{-4}$
at a $5\,\%$ accuracy target), $\alpha_{\mathrm{van}}=10^{-8}$ (death), and
$\alpha_{\mathrm{birth}}=2\alpha_{\mathrm{cond}}$ (nucleation seed).  The
ordering gives hysteresis with no flip-flop channel: birth at
$2\alpha_{\mathrm{cond}}$, loss of independence only below
$\alpha_{\mathrm{cond}}$, death only below $\alpha_{\mathrm{van}}$.

\section{Discretisation of the non-conservative slots}
\label{sec:disc}
% =====================================================================

One Riemann solve per face produces a wave decomposition, from which the code
takes \emph{two different exits}.

\paragraph{Conserved slots} ($\rho,\rho\uvec,\rho E,m_1,m_2$) receive a single
recovered interface flux $F^\star = F(\mathbf{U}_L) + \mathsf{A}^-\Delta Q$,
which telescopes exactly to the fluctuation update, so CAMR's ordinary
flux-difference path and its AMReX flux register are used unchanged.

\paragraph{Non-conserved slots} ($\alpha_1,\mathcal{E}_1,\mathcal{E}_2$) get a
face flux of \emph{exactly zero} and a direct fluctuation deposit,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha_1}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_i
   \;=\;-\,\frac{\mathsf{A}^+\Delta Q_{i-1/2}
                + \mathsf{A}^-\Delta Q_{i+1/2}}{\Delta x},
\end{equation}
and likewise for $\mathcal{E}_1,\mathcal{E}_2$.  Because both exits are fed by
the same wave structure they cannot disagree about what happened at the face,
which is what makes a uniform-pressure, uniform-velocity interface drift
without generating spurious waves \emph{by construction} rather than by
cancellation between two independent discretisations.

\subsection{Wave structure}

Three waves per face (HLLC, specialised to the six-equation system following
Pelanti 2022).  Outer speeds are Davis estimates using each side's own mixture
sound speed \src{PS\_hllc.H:375--376}:
\begin{equation}
  S_L = \min\!\bigl(u_L-c_L,\ u_R-c_R\bigr),
  \qquad
  S_R = \max\!\bigl(u_L+c_L,\ u_R+c_R\bigr),
\end{equation}
with the Wallis frozen mixture speed \src{PS\_hllc.H:303}
\begin{equation}
  c^2 = Y_1c_1^2 + Y_2c_2^2,
  \qquad
  Y_1 = \frac{\alpha_1\rho_1}{\rho},\quad Y_2 = 1-Y_1 .
  \label{eq:cmix}
\end{equation}
``Frozen'' means no mass transfer during the acoustic response; the much
slower equilibrium speed belongs to the relaxation operator, which is applied
separately.  \code{CAMR.ps\_cmix\_model} (default $0$) also offers
$c=\max(c_1,c_2)$ and the Wood equilibrium form.

The contact speed follows from the mixture mass and momentum balance
\src{PS\_hllc.H:386}:
\begin{equation}
  S_M = \frac{P_R-P_L + \rho_Lu_L(S_L-u_L) - \rho_Ru_R(S_R-u_R)}
             {\rho_L(S_L-u_L) - \rho_R(S_R-u_R)} .
\end{equation}
$\alpha_1$ is advected at $S_M$ --- the local contact velocity at that face,
not a cell-averaged mixture velocity --- which is what preserves the contact.

\subsection{Star states}

A \emph{single} contraction ratio, shared by both phases
\src{PS\_hllc.H:491--495}:
\begin{equation}
  r_K = \frac{S_K-u_K}{S_K-S_M},
  \qquad
  m_1^\star = r_K\,m_1,
  \qquad
  m_2^\star = r_K\,m_2 .
  \label{eq:rk}
\end{equation}
Because both partial masses scale by the same number, their ratio --- and hence
the composition --- is untouched by the acoustic compression, which is what
lets the volume fraction survive the fan.  The per-phase star energies are
\src{PS\_hllc.H:515--519}
\begin{equation}
  E_k^\star = E_k + (S_M-u_K)\Bigl(S_M + \frac{P_{e,k}}{q_k}\Bigr),
  \qquad
  q_k = \rho_k\,(S_K-u_K),
  \label{eq:estar}
\end{equation}
with $P_{e,k}=P$ (the \emph{mixture} pressure) by default and $P_{e,k}=P_k$
under \code{CAMR.ps\_pk\_energy\_flux}$=1$; the two coincide at mechanical
equilibrium.  The asymmetric pairing of a mixture pressure with a per-phase
density in \eqref{eq:estar} is COTT's HLLC\_v form --- a validated choice, not
an obvious one.

\paragraph{Failure is refusal, not repair.}
A non-finite intermediate, a vanishing denominator or a non-positive star
density makes the face return \code{false}, and that face alone falls back to a
local Lax--Friedrichs flux.  Nothing is clamped to make the star state exist.

\section{Two known departures of the implementation from the model}
\label{sec:caveats}
% =====================================================================

Recorded here because they are properties of the code, not of the model, and
both were measured on 13 August 2026.

\paragraph{(i) The mass-transfer target does not travel the step's path.}
The step applies \eqref{eq:E1rule} with the carrier \eqref{eq:carrier}, but
$\delta m_{\mathrm{eq}}$ in \eqref{eq:mt} is computed at \emph{fixed}
$\alpha_1$ with a \emph{mean} carrier regardless of $w$.  Along fixed
$\alpha_1$, $\partial\rho_1/\partial m = 1/\alpha_1$, whereas along
\eqref{eq:E1rule} it is exactly zero, so the target's Gibbs sensitivity carries
a $1/\alpha_1$ amplification the step does not have.  Consequently the
non-overshoot property of \eqref{eq:mt} does not hold, which is the measured
cause of aborts at the $T=1$~K bracket end when the coexistence gate is opened.
\code{CAMR.ps\_mt\_target}$=1$ makes the two consistent (default $0$ retains
the legacy behaviour).

\paragraph{(ii) The relaxation rates carry a hidden morphology assumption.}
Both $\theta$ in \eqref{eq:thermal} and $\tau_{\!m}$ in \eqref{eq:mt} are
global constants, but the physical rates are set by the interfacial area the
two phases share \emph{within} a cell.  A dispersed mixture and a numerically
smeared material contact can carry identical
$(\alpha_1,\rho_1,\rho_2,e_1,e_2,P,T_1,T_2)$ and still require $\theta$
differing by three and a half orders of magnitude.  The six-equation state
carries no interfacial-area variable, so no function of it can distinguish the
two cases.  The coexistence gate of \S\ref{sec:sources} acts as a crude binary
proxy for that distinction.  By contrast the sound speed \eqref{eq:cmix},
which embeds the same assumption, was measured to be insensitive to it at the
$3\,\%$ level, because $S_L,S_R$ need only \emph{bound} the fan.
