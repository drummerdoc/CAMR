# Heat-limited bubble growth: a finite-rate nucleation-and-growth source for the liquid-dominant cell

Status: plan for the maintainer's decisions; no code. `[DECIDE-G1]` (24 Sept 2026) opened this note; every further `[DECIDE-G*]` is the maintainer's. The rules are `AGENT_HANDBOOK.md` §1; predictions are written per commit before the runs, in this file, and kept whatever they turn out to be.

## 1. The observation this answers

ECCSEL Test 19 (cold, 10.2 °C), at every near-exit sensor, has three time scales after the rarefaction: a fast drop to a plateau near the onset level (29–30 bar), a *second* decline over 1.5 ms at 8 cm and 2.5 ms at 28 cm down to 18–22 bar, and a recovery over 8 ms to 25–27 bar. Every model available to us does the second decline in one step (`VERIFICATION.md` §6.8, `Exec/CO2_PipeDepress/report/fig_hrmscan.pdf`): the default and the HEM because the nucleator seeds α_birth = 0.04 of vapour at saturation density in a few steps and a cell with 4 % vapour has a mixture sound speed of tens of m/s; the HRM probe at θ ≥ 1 ms because no vapour exists for the first 0.1 ms and the liquid rarefaction runs to the vent floor (the p_amb scan of 23 Sept: p_amb sets the floor height and nothing after it; θ alone sets the late level, 2.5 bar per ms of θ, at every sensor). The θ = 0.25 ms probe is the exception that shows the shape of the answer: its second decline takes 5 ms, but it has no recovery, because a constant θ does not slow down as the superheat is consumed. The data want a source that is fast at onset, slower as vapour appears, and dying as the superheat goes. No constant relaxation time is all three; a conduction-limited growth law is.

## 2. The law

A vapour bubble in superheated liquid grows by conduction of latent heat to its surface. The Plesset–Zwick asymptote (Plesset & Zwick 1954; Scriven 1959 gives the same leading term) is

$$R(t) = 2\,\mathrm{Ja}\sqrt{\frac{3\,a_\ell\,t}{\pi}}, \qquad \mathrm{Ja} = \frac{\rho_\ell\,c_{p\ell}\,(T_\ell - T_{\mathrm{sat}}(P))}{\rho_v\,h_{fg}},$$

with $a_\ell = k_\ell/(\rho_\ell c_{p\ell})$ the liquid thermal diffusivity. Written without a time origin, $R\dot R = 6\,\mathrm{Ja}^2 a_\ell/\pi$, so for $n$ bubbles per unit volume, $R = (3\alpha_v/4\pi n)^{1/3}$, the volumetric evaporation rate is

$$\Gamma = \rho_v\, n\, 4\pi R^2 \dot R = 24\,\rho_v\, a_\ell\, \mathrm{Ja}^2\, n\, R \;\propto\; n^{2/3}\,\alpha_v^{1/3}\,\mathrm{Ja}^2 .$$

Every input is the liquid's own state or a table value at the liquid temperature ($\rho_v = \rho_{v,\mathrm{sat}}(T_\ell)$ from `co2_sat_state`, $h_{fg}$ from the EOS at $T_\ell$, $T_{\mathrm{sat}}(P)$ by inverting `Psat_of_T`, $a_\ell$ a literature constant, §4), which is the X-40 constraint for a rate acting below `alpha_cond`: the trace vapour's own intensive state is never queried. Three behaviours are built in: at onset Ja is large (Test 19: 15 K of superheat, Ja ≈ 1.7); $\alpha_v^{1/3}$ keeps the rate finite from the first seed; and as $T_\ell \to T_{\mathrm{sat}}(P)$ the rate vanishes quadratically, which is the recovery.

Exact integral at frozen Ja. With $y = \alpha_v^{2/3}$ the ODE $\dot\alpha_v = \Gamma/\rho_v$ becomes $\dot y = K$, a constant:

$$\alpha_v^{2/3}(t+\Delta t) = \alpha_v^{2/3}(t) + K\,\Delta t, \qquad K = 16\, a_\ell\, \mathrm{Ja}^2 \left(\frac{3 n^2}{4\pi}\right)^{1/3} \ [\mathrm{s}^{-1}].$$

So the per-step update is closed-form, monotone and unconditionally stable in α; no Newton, no cap. The only explicit part is Ja frozen over the step, and it is not stiff: with $\Delta t \approx 2.5\,\mu$s (CFL 0.25 at 6 mm) and $\alpha_v = 0.01$, $\Delta\alpha \approx 2\times10^{-6}$ per step, so the liquid's pressure moves by $K_\ell\Delta\alpha/\alpha_\ell \approx 400$ Pa per step against a window that is bars wide. The nucleator's gain-10 loop (`MODEL_AND_ALGORITHM.md` §5.5) does not arise.

Numbers (Test 19: $a_\ell \approx 4.7\times10^{-8}$ m²/s, Ja ≈ 1.7): $K = 6.3$ s⁻¹ at $n = 10^{10}$ m⁻³, ×4.6 per decade of $n$. Time for the vapour to reach 1 %: 7.4 ms at $10^{10}$, 1.6 ms at $10^{11}$, 0.34 ms at $10^{12}$. One decade in $n$ moves the second-decline time scale by a factor 4.6; $n$ is a physical number (active nucleation sites per volume), not a fitted time.

The seed is irrelevant. Nucleation at the CNT onset creates bubbles at the critical radius $2\sigma/\Delta P \approx 13$ nm, i.e. $\alpha_0 \approx 10^{-13}$ at $n = 10^{10}$, below `alpha_vanish` ($10^{-8}$). Since $y$ grows linearly the time lost to a larger seed is $\alpha_0^{2/3}/K$: for $\alpha_0 = 10^{-6}$ (`ps_growth_seed`, default) that is 16 µs at $n = 10^{10}$. Any seed between `alpha_vanish` and $10^{-4}$ gives the same trajectory to within the plot line.

What $n$ is, physically. Two populations: heterogeneous sites (wall, impurities) that do not depend on the state, and homogeneous nuclei, $n_{\mathrm{hom}} = \int J_{\mathrm{CNT}}\,dt$, which the code already knows how to evaluate ($J$ of `ps_flash_margin_cnt`) but which needs a per-cell accumulator to integrate. Near the critical point $\sigma \to 0$ and $J$ explodes, so $n_{\mathrm{hom}}$ dominates warm; far from it the CNT onset sits at 63 % undershoot against a measured 9–10 %, so cold nucleation is heterogeneous and $n$ is a wall property. This is the same cold/warm split as the onset cap (R-17). The first version (§3) takes $n$ uniform per run and *measures* $n$ on Tests 19 and 24; whether the ratio is the CNT ratio is what decides if §1.4 of `FUTURE_WORK.md` (a transported $n$) is needed.

Ja warm. At Test 24 conditions (35.8 °C) the onset undershoot is 2.3 % of $P_{\mathrm{sat}}$, about 1 K of superheat, with $\rho_v/\rho_\ell \approx 1/3$ and $h_{fg} \approx 100$ kJ/kg: Ja ≈ 0.15, so Ja² is ~130× smaller than cold. At equal $n$ the law is *slower* warm, while the data (D ≲ 2 mm warm vs 0.2 m cold, θ = 0.14 vs 2.5 ms in the reference) want it faster. The law can only be right for both tests if $n$ is orders of magnitude larger warm, which is what the CNT rate says it is. This is the plan's falsifier, stated before any run: **if a uniform $n$ that fits Test 19 leaves Test 24 slower than today's default, and no $n$ within the homogeneous CNT estimate fixes it, the law is wrong or incomplete, and the note says so.**

## 3. The pieces

**G-a. Where it lives.** In the flash kernel (`hem::ps_flash_source_cell`, `hem_flash.H`), which is already the one operator allowed to act on a liquid-dominant cell whose vapour is Absent or Corridor, and already injects newborn mass at the vapour's saturation density with its saturation energy ((E.1) backwards, the latent heat taken from the liquid). Nothing about routing, presence, carriers or the reproject changes. The kernel gets two sub-operators in place of its one:

- *Nucleate* (window-gated, as now): the CNT/capped onset window $w_T w_{\mathrm{meta}} w_\alpha$ decides eligibility exactly as today; what changes is the amount. Today: $\Delta\alpha = s\beta(\alpha_{\mathrm{birth}} - \alpha_v)$ toward 0.04. Under the key: if $\alpha_v <$ `ps_growth_seed`, set it to the seed (one event, at the window's implicit root as now); otherwise nothing — growth owns it.
- *Grow* (superheat-gated, new): for a liquid-dominant cell with $\alpha_v \ge$ seed and $\alpha_v <$ `alpha_cond`, if $T_\ell > T_{\mathrm{sat}}(P)$, apply the closed-form update above at frozen Ja, injecting $\delta m = \rho_{v,\mathrm{sat}}(T_\ell)\,\Delta\alpha$ through the existing birth path. The window plays no part: a bubble grows whenever the liquid is superheated, including after the pressure has recovered above the onset band — that is the recovery. The fractional limiter (`valid_at` bisection) still bounds the step; the per-step mass cap (half the donor) is unreachable at these rates.
- *Hand-over*: at $\alpha_v \ge$ `alpha_cond` the vapour is promoted (§4.5) and X3 owns the cell, as now. The kernel logs $\dot\alpha$ on its last step and X3 its first (`[PS-GROW]` diagnostic line under `ps_mt_diag`) so the rate discontinuity at the hand-over is measured, not assumed. `[DECIDE-G3]` says what to do about it.
- Condensation (vapour-dominant cells) and the both-Independent cell: untouched.

**G-b. Keys.** `CAMR.ps_growth_n` [m⁻³], default 0 = off = today's kernel, byte-inert by the battery. `CAMR.ps_growth_seed` [-], default 1e-6, read only when $n > 0$. `CAMR.ps_growth_a_l` [m²/s], default 4.7e-8 (CO₂ liquid at 280 K, from $k_\ell \approx 0.10$ W/mK, $c_{p\ell} \approx 2.5$ kJ/kgK, $\rho_\ell \approx 860$; a literature constant, §4 records the source; a $T$-dependence is a later refinement). Lifecycle per the handbook: temporary → inert → gated → default flip (only if the battery and both ECCSEL tests say so) → retirement.

**G-c. What the EOS must supply.** $T_{\mathrm{sat}}(P)$: bisection on `Psat_of_T` over the dome (12 evaluations; or the Span–Wagner auxiliary equation already in `pipe_depress.py`, inverted once per cell). $h_{fg}(T_\ell)$: $h_v - h_\ell$ at the saturation densities of `co2_sat_state` through the phase API (one query each), or the Clausius–Clapeyron form $h_{fg} = T\,(1/\rho_v - 1/\rho_\ell)\,dP_{\mathrm{sat}}/dT$ from the same table, which needs no EOS call. `[DECIDE-G4]`. $c_{p\ell}$: from the liquid's own query (the API gives $c_v$ and the derivatives; $c_p$ at the liquid state is available where `cv_of` is), or folded into $a_\ell$ as a constant. Either way no query of the vapour.

**G-d. Restart.** No new state. Nothing to gate beyond the existing checkpoint round trip, which must stay bit-identical at $n = 0$ and consistent (same trajectory from a checkpoint as from $t = 0$) at $n > 0$; the latter is one run pair on Test 19.

**G-e. 2-D.** The kernel is per cell; nothing directional. The XC2D before/after pair at $n = 0$ is the 2-D witness (handbook §4); no 2-D run at $n > 0$ is part of this plan.

## 5. Decisions

`[DECIDE-G1]` Go ahead: **taken, 24 Sept.**
`[DECIDE-G2]` Where the law acts: below `alpha_cond` only, inside the flash kernel, X3 untouched (recommended for the first version: smallest change, no new state, the SRT rate above `alpha_cond` is what it is today and the hand-over is measured) — or also replace X3's `rM` in the boiling direction by the same law (one more branch in `rates`, needs the vapour's $\rho$ only as the receiver, consistent with X-40 since above `alpha_cond` the vapour is Independent). Recommendation: the first, with the second as commit 3b if the hand-over log shows a rate jump of more than 10× at `alpha_cond`.
`[DECIDE-G3]` The hand-over at `alpha_cond`: promote and let X3 take over (recommended; measured by the `[PS-GROW]` line) — or keep growing in the kernel up to a higher fraction (would put the kernel in mass transfer's territory, against §5.5's "nucleation only").
`[DECIDE-G4]` $h_{fg}$: Clausius–Clapeyron from the saturation table (recommended: no EOS call, consistent with the table the onset already uses) or two phase-API enthalpy queries.
`[DECIDE-G5]` The seed default: 1e-6 (recommended; 100× `alpha_vanish`, 16 µs of lost time at $n = 10^{10}$) or `alpha_vanish` itself.
`[DECIDE-G6]` Whether the plateau-holding behaviour of the implicit nucleator (the front cell held at the top of the window band) is kept at $n > 0$: yes (recommended: it is what puts the first level where the data have it, and the growth law does not touch pressure directly) — or the seed event happens once and the window is then ignored for that cell.

## 6. What this plan does not do

No transported quantity; no wall-site boundary source; no $\theta(\Sigma)$; no change to the onset law, the cap, the mechanical or thermal relaxation, condensation, or X3 (under G2's recommendation). It is the smallest change that gives the liquid-dominant cell a finite, state-dependent, self-limiting rate, and it is designed to be refuted by two tests at opposite ends of the dome.

## 7. Record of the runs (24 Sept 2026; Test 19, PR EOS, 4 m / 640 cells / 10 ms, run in the VM)

**Commit 2 alone** (growth below `alpha_cond`, X3's SRT above; `_to_delete/runs_grow_2only/`): the 8 cm minimum rises with $n$ (1.0 / 5.8 / 17.1 / 18.2 bar at $10^{10\ldots13}$) and the 28 cm trough from 4 to 30 bar, so the rebound after the first drop is $n\,\mathrm{Ja}^2$ as §2 says; but from ~0.5 ms every $n$ follows the default, because at the hand-over the kernel's rate is ~3 /s against X3's ~80 /s (30×, past G2's 10× trigger). The commit-3 prediction "first level unchanged" was wrong: with the 4 % seed gone the 28 cm level moves with $n$ (23.7 → 32.8 bar at +0.5 ms); the plateau is the growth-expansion balance, not a separate holder ([DECIDE-G6] as it actually is).

**Commit 3b** (the law also as X3's boiling rate; `runs/g19_n1e1{1,2,3}_pr`, `report/fig_gscan3b.pdf`): predictions wrong. The pressure after the first drop is stationary for 10 ms at an $n$-dependent level (8 cm: 16 / 22 / 27 bar; 28 cm: 22 / 27 / 32 bar for $n = 10^{11}/10^{12}/10^{13}$); no second decline and no recovery at any $n$. At $n = 10^{13}$ the turn-around lands on the measured plateau at both sensors (27 vs 29; 32 vs 30) with no floor and no spike. A conduction-limited bulk rate answers the *first* feature (the rebound into the first drop) and cannot produce the second decline. Exit diagnostic from the plotfiles: in every model, default and growth alike, the exit is at its maximum mass flux and 20–40 % vapour by 0.2 ms; the reference's own reading of its Figs. 8–9 is that the second decline is the depressurization *between* flashing initiation and the outlet choking, so its duration is the outlet's time to choke.

**[DECIDE-V1], the disc-opening law** (`CAMR.ps_bc_vent_open_time`, `_open_exp`, `_open_frac0`; inert at 0 on FF/N1/N4/N1b and the battery; `runs/v19_o{1,3,5}ms`, `v19_o3ms_b{5,7,85}`, `report/fig_vscan.pdf`). The vent's fan is marched a second time to the point where $G = \beta(t)\,G_{\max}$ on its subsonic branch (a throat of area $\beta A$ chokes at the fan's own $G_{\max}$). Prediction: the second decline's duration scales with $\tau_{\mathrm{open}}$. **Refuted.** For every $\beta_0$ and $\tau$ the near sensors hold the flashing plateau (33.8 bar on PR) until the disc is fully open and then drop in one step to the choked level (20 bar), exactly as the instant-opening default does at $t = 0$; the step only moves to $t = \tau$. The reason is in the fan itself: $G(P)$ is flat at the choke, so the pipe-face pressure is insensitive to $\beta$ until $\beta \to 1$; any monotone area law gives plateau-then-step. $\beta_0 = 0.85$ gives a 3-bar drift over 3 ms before the step, the most the law can do. The disc's opening time is therefore not what sets the second decline either.

**What remains.** The reference's HEM at 8 cm (digitised, Fig. 4 of the ECCSEL note) shows a slow decline, 37 → 27 bar over 10 ms, with no undershoot: the equilibrium two-phase rarefaction fan passing the sensor at the mixture sound speed. Our θ = 0.25 ms HRM probe, the closest of ours to equilibrium, is the only run of ours with a slow decline (30 → 25 over 5 ms). The measured second decline (29 → 18.6 over 1.5 ms at 8 cm, 30 → 22 over 3 ms at 28 cm) is faster and deeper than HEM's and is followed by the recovery HEM lacks. The open question is now stated as: why do our runs not show the equilibrium fan at all — after the first drop they are already at or below the choked level (21 bar at 8 cm against the measured 29) and can only rise — and which part of the source chain puts them there. Candidates, not yet tested: the exit's choking state (our vent chokes the cell's frozen/Wood mixture; the reference's BBC chokes on entropy and quality); the near-exit cells' equilibrium being reached before the fan can form (the fan needs a mixture that softens *gradually* along the pipe, i.e. a transfer rate that is fast enough to follow equilibrium but not so fast that the exit cells jump to the choked state in one step).

**[DECIDE-V2], the vent's choking criterion** (`CAMR.ps_bc_vent_choke` = fan | bernoulli; inert at `fan` on FF (85 BCRX lines) and N4; `runs/probe19{,b}`, `b19_bern`, `b19_bern_n13`, `report/fig_v2scan.pdf`). The probe first: with today's fan the exit cell reaches a Wood Mach number of 0.98 at step 99 and 1.46 at step 299 (1 ms) at 5.5 bar — the outlet is over-expanded past its own sonic point, the failure the reference describes for a plain pressure ghost (its §4). The cause is the simple-wave march: the Riemann-invariant velocity reaches the sound speed at a far lower pressure than the steady-energy velocity does (a gas: 0.28 p₀ against 0.53 p₀), and a quasi-steady outlet obeys the steady relation. `bernoulli` keeps the fan's thermodynamic path (per-phase isentropes, HEM re-closure) but takes u² = u_N² + 2(h_N − h) and ends where u = c on the path, the reference's Bernoulli choking point; a cell already sonic gets no fan. Prediction: exit Mach ≤ 1 and a higher exit pressure (firm); a higher 8 cm level and a slower second decline (uncertain). Result: the firm part holds (exit cell Mw 0.5 at 1 ms, 14 bar instead of 5.5); the sensors do not move: 8 cm and 28 cm within 0.5 bar of the fan run at every time, with or without the growth law (`b19_bern_n13` ≡ `g19_n1e13_pr` to 1 bar). The near-exit pressure level is set by the interior two-phase state after onset, not by the outlet, so the choking criterion is not what the second decline is about either. `bernoulli` is nevertheless the physically right end for a quasi-steady vent and removes a supersonic boundary cell; whether it becomes the default is a separate decision (it changes no witness at `fan`, and would move the vent references).

**Standing after four refutations** (θ alone, growth alone, disc opening, choke criterion): what moves the near-exit level after onset is the transfer rate in the first 0.3 ms (n Ja² in the growth law, or the seed in the nucleator); nothing tried makes the pressure at 8 cm fall a second time after the plateau or recover afterwards. Every configuration is stationary or monotone after 0.5 ms. Untested and left open: thermal non-equilibrium between the phases after onset (X3's θ against the reference's pT-relaxed HRM*, which holds T_v = T_l), the closed 3 m / 4 m domain against the 60 m pipe (the reflected wave is not the issue at 10 ms, but the fan's tail is set by the whole liquid column), and friction/heat transfer (both small at 8 cm in 10 ms by the estimate of the ECCSEL note).
